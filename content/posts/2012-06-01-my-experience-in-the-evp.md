Last May, from the 7th to the 11th, I used the hours Liferay gives its employees to take part in a volunteer program. Now that I'm back in the office, I think the best way to tell you what those days were like is a blog post.

First, for those who don't know: EVP stands for Employee Volunteer Program. It shows that Liferay clearly bets on **motivating its employees**, with a Corporate Social Responsibility policy that helps us help others. To me this is super motivating, and many Spanish companies should learn from it and use it.

Second, now that my EVP is over, I have to admit I loved the experience. Everything I taught, a lot or a little, will be used directly by people who were looking for help. Being able to help them felt good, and it left me wanting to do it again.

For my EVP I chose to work with [COCEMFECLM](http://www.cocemfeclm.org/), a non-governmental organization in Castilla-La Mancha, headquartered in the city of Toledo. Its main job is to work with people with physical and organic disabilities, to make sure the **principle of equal opportunities** is met and to **achieve full integration in education, work and society, removing every kind of barrier**.

I found out about the organization through a friend who volunteers there, and I'm deeply grateful to him. He told me what he did there and I thought: "why can't I help too?", "I could help somehow, with technology or without it". So I set up a meeting with COCEMFECLM, where I explained my ideas to see if they fit their projects.

Once there, Inés Escudero, who is in charge of the projects in the women's area, thanked me very much for wanting to help. She told me they had a really nice project about **promoting entrepreneurship among women with disabilities in rural areas**. It consisted (and still consists) of a series of tutoring and mentoring processes to create, in the future, new businesses that let them create their own self-employment.

COCEMFECLM points out that "_women with disabilities suffer the highest unemployment rates, reaching almost 60 percent of their active population, besides suffering multiple discriminations and being victims of every kind of violence_", so "_it is important that public administrations design equity policies, prioritizing the groups that are most vulnerable and sensitive to the crisis, such as women and men with disabilities_".

That was where my help fit perfectly: showing them the advantages that technology offers to these businesses. Everything was already in motion.

That day we prepared the ingredients for what finally came out of the oven as the course "**Learning to Be an Entrepreneur: Technology at the Service of Entrepreneurship**". It would be 5 days of training to help this disadvantaged group improve their technology skills and promote self-employment.

We decided the course would cover:

- **how to find information and communicate** on the Internet, mainly through good use of search engines, instant messaging and email.
- how to take advantage of the new business model that **the cloud** means for SMEs and the self-employed, through **collaboration tools** such as Google Docs, or file sharing tools such as DropBox.
- how to **use social networks professionally** to increase the visibility of the products and services a business offers, with Twitter, Facebook and LinkedIn.
- the **use of blogs** as a communication tool.
- Finally, we decided that the students should close the cycle by **creating a blog**.

Once the course started, I found myself in front of 8 women and 2 men who were very eager to learn. They told me they had hesitated to sign up, because they weren't sure what use they could make of it. But as the days went by, I realized how much interest they were putting in. They didn't even remind me about the break in the middle of the day!

The rest of the course went very smoothly. The students were an ideal audience: interruptions were minimal and attention was at its maximum.

The experience also got quite a lot of media coverage in the city of Toledo, and I would even venture to say across Castilla-La Mancha. On the second to last day, regional media came to a press conference. Speaking at it, besides me, were María Teresa Novillo, the Director of the [Instituto de la Mujer de Castilla-La Mancha](http://www.institutomujer.jccm.es/) (the region's Women's Institute), and Julio Roldán Perezagua, the President of COCEMFECLM.
