I was talking with my colleague, friend and Oracle expert Moisés Espinosa ([https://moisesespinosa.wordpress.com](https://moisesespinosa.wordpress.com), [@moikmeg](http://twitter.com/#!/moikmeg)) about [Oracle's acquisition of Fatwire](http://www.oracle.com/us/corporate/press/418753). Two conversations came out of it.

The first one was about how Oracle keeps buying companies in the web development space. First it bought the legendary Sun Microsystems, and with it Java. Then it bought BEA, and with it the Weblogic application server. Now it is buying Fatwire, whose web portal product is one of the main tools in the portal management market.

It looks like Oracle is getting into portal management. I don't know if it is testing the market or taking a position in it, but one thing seems clear: the sector is going to feel it. With a mega-corporation like Oracle behind the product, Oracle can grow strategically by offering its customers a whole set of services. They could go from the physical level of the databases up to one of the highest layers of content management, a web portal like Fatwire, deployed, of course, on its own Oracle Weblogic application server.

It also makes you think about the future of Oracle Portal now that Fatwire is part of the family, because both products could end up sharing the same space in the market. At first glance, we could conclude that the same thing will happen as with Oracle Application Server (OAS), which is slowly making way for Oracle's other big acquisition, the former BEA Weblogic.

*What do you think about the future of Oracle Portal?*

The second conversation was about the future of the competitors, both commercial and Open Source: Liferay, Vignette, Magnolia, ...

*How will the competitors face the arrival of a megacorporation with an already established product like Fatwire?*
